Abstract

Prompt injection is the dominant unsolved security risk for LLM agents, and the community now agrees that robustness must be measured against adaptive attackers rather than fixed benchmark injections. Many adaptive red teamers exist, and most are built to maximize attack success rate (ASR). Yet ASR counts how often a target breaks, not in how many different ways. A red teamer can achieve a high ASR by reusing a few techniques that work, leaving the others untested and absent from the attacks used to harden models. We present AutoPI, an adaptive red teamer designed to find many different ways to break a target, not just to break it often. AutoPI uses PIAtlas, a taxonomy of prompt-injection techniques, both to guide its search and to measure coverage. For each scenario, an attacker LLM writes an injection for every sub-technique in the taxonomy and then reworks the ones that fail using feedback from an LLM judge. The method is black-box and training-free, it plugs into existing benchmarks in place of their fixed attack templates, and it stays up to date as the taxonomy evolves. We evaluate AutoPI on AgentDojo and IPI Arena against open-weight and proprietary target models. Alongside ASR, we report technique coverage, the fraction of taxonomy classes, techniques, and sub-techniques that yield at least one successful injection. On AgentDojo, AutoPI finds a successful injection against Qwen3.6-35B in all six classes of the taxonomy and for more than 90% of its techniques. Against Claude Haiku 5.5, the most robust target we evaluate, it achieves between 20% and 50% higher ASR and between 25% and 400% higher class-level coverage than recent state-of-the-art adaptive red teamers, while requiring no training. We release our code and the generated attacks to support reproducible, coverage-aware evaluation.

How AutoPI works

Working injections are rare, and PIAtlas conjectures that they lie in regions, one per technique. Exploration takes the search near every region, and exploitation closes in on each.

  • Initialize

    An optional fixed seed injection, tried first with no generator call.

  • Explore

    A sweep over the taxonomy. The generator LLM writes one short injection line for every sub-technique.

  • Exploit

    Local refinement. A line that fails is reworked into variants of the same construction, using feedback from an LLM judge.

The phases are building blocks. A researcher can skip, resize, or repeat any of them to compose a search strategy.

Results at a glance

AgentDojo against Qwen3.6-35B, every attacker ranked on five criteria. PIMiner and CtH are trained before the attack. AutoPI is not.

  • The most robust target

    Against Claude Haiku 5.5, AutoPI-Full breaks 6 of 97 scenarios, against 5 for CtH and for PIMiner. Its successes cover five of the six classes, against four, two and one for the three baselines. A robust target leaves few successes, four to six per attacker here.

  • A second benchmark, unchanged

    On IPI Arena the engine runs unchanged and nothing is retrained. AutoPI-Full breaks 14 of 41 scenarios, against 16 for PIMiner and 15 for CtH, and 10 against 9 and 8 on the 21 scenarios outside PIMiner's training set.

  • Short lines suffice

    The injections with which the sweep breaks a scenario have a median of 31 words, against 70 for AutoDojo, 112 for PIMiner and 219 for CtH. A sweep injection is a short line built on one or two constructions.

Run it

The repository holds the engine, its search strategies, and the AgentDojo and IPI Arena sources it runs on.

Install and run

The target, the generator and the judge are plain LLM endpoints.

git clone https://github.com/stevendamianakis/autopi
cd autopi
pip install -e . -e benchmarks/agentdojo

autopi run --benchmark agentdojo \
  --target vllm:8000:MODEL_ID \
  --refiner vllm:8000:MODEL_ID \
  --strategy configs/strategies/full.yaml \
  --logdir ./runs

In the code, the generator is called the refiner.
